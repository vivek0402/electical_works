# Design: Making SVKML intelligent

Mode: Startup (intrapreneurship: a paid client build)

## Problem Statement
The office staff at SVKML (a lighting service partner in Visakhapatnam that works with Wipro, Havells and other companies) lose time and make mistakes in three places:
1. **Typing in complaints.** Complaints arrive by phone, WhatsApp and the companies' emails, and staff re-type the site, contact, phone, product and fault. Today's sheet holds lines like *"Punjab National Bank, Gudivada, 15watt Drivers 18nos not working"*.
2. **Stock running out.** A technician needs a driver or PCB that isn't on the shelf, so the complaint waits and the site needs a second visit. Meanwhile other spares sit on the shelf for months.
3. **Chasing pending work.** Complaints stay open too long, defective pieces sit in the store without a return batch, and dispatched batches go unanswered by the company for weeks. Nobody notices until the customer or the company calls.

"Intelligent" here means the app notices and suggests: it fills forms, warns about mistakes, lists what needs chasing today, and learns reorder levels and fault patterns from the data the app already records. It is not a chatbot.

## Demand Evidence
- The user, who builds and sells this app to the client, picked all three pains above (D2: "All the above").
- The legacy sheets in `data/legacy/` show the cost of today's way of working: complaints tracked by hand with free-text status ("material need to check", "ramu 02/02/26"), and phone numbers that are truncated (`88410`) or have a digit missing (`996394502`).
- **Gap:** no number from the client yet on how many complaints arrive per day or how many hours go into re-typing. The assignment below closes this gap.

## Status Quo
Staff use Excel sheets plus WhatsApp and phone calls today. The new app (stock ledger, complaints, defectives, about 850 tests) replaces the sheets but is still "type everything in by hand". It already has some intelligence: look-alike item codes (`Item.search_key`, so O/0, I/1 and hyphens match), low-stock lists, menu badges and dashboards.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
- **Users:** the office staff (role `OFFICE`), who enter stock, file complaints and record visits, and the owner (role `ADMIN`), who wants to know what is late and what is costing money.
- **Narrowest wedge:** the **morning "Today" list**. One screen (plus an optional email) showing what needs chasing today: overdue complaints, batches the company hasn't answered, pieces waiting to be batched, and stock needed by open complaints that is running low. It works from day one on an empty database, uses no AI and costs nothing to run.

## Constraints
- Django 5.2 + PostgreSQL 16, with no build step (Tabler + Tom Select are vendored). Every write goes through an app's `services.py`. The stock ledger is append-only, and changes are cancelled with `undo_entry`.
- **Lock order** (top of `apps/defectives/services.py`) applies to any new service that combines complaints, batches, pieces and stock.
- No company is special (`tasks/lessons.md`): rules and settings are per company, never hard-coded for Wipro.
- **Every screen gets a clickable mockup and user approval before it is built**, and every feature ships with demo data (`seed_service_demo`).
- The client install starts empty (change request CR1, in progress).
- Cloud AI spend is **not approved yet**. The user will ask the client (D3).
- Small team: 2–3 office users and a few technicians. Alert fatigue is a real risk.

## Premises (agreed, D5)
1. **Suggest, never act.** Every smart feature proposes something and a person confirms it. The app never posts stock, changes a complaint's status or messages a customer by itself.
2. **Free rules and statistics come first.** Cloud AI is a separate, optional tier with an on/off switch and a monthly cost cap, built only after the client says yes.
3. **Useful from day one.** Tier 1 works on an empty database. Tier 2 features switch on by themselves once there is enough history, and until then they show "learning: needs N more weeks of data".
4. **Legacy Excel can be imported once** to give tier 2 a head start.

## Prior Art (research, 2026-10-19)
- **ERPNext** reorders when *projected* stock falls below the reorder level (lead time × average daily use) and has a Stock Ageing report. **Odoo** uses min/max rules and offers ML forecasting only through paid apps. **InvenTree and Snipe-IT** have low-stock alerts only. Nobody at this scale does more than rules plus simple statistics.
- **Helpdesks (Frappe, Odoo)** use SLA deadlines with colour states and escalation. Their lesson: at this team size, send one morning digest, not many alerts.
- **Spare-part demand is intermittent.** Classify each item with Syntetos–Boylan (ADI 1.32 / CV² 0.49) and use Croston, SBA or TSB only when there is enough history; otherwise average use × lead time + safety stock. Keep the zero-demand weeks in the series. Count only real consumption (visit parts, OUT to a site or technician), not transfers, adjustments, undone entries or moves to the defective store.
- **LLM intake must stop at a draft.** Models invent or swap phone numbers and look-alike codes, so every extracted value is checked against the masters and the raw message is kept.
- **Libraries (all MIT, Apache or BSD):**
  - pg_trgm (built into Postgres) and RapidFuzz for fuzzy matching.
  - statsforecast for SBA and TSB (optional: a hand-written SBA is about 20 lines and avoids the numba dependency).
  - pydantic-ai or instructor for structured LLM extraction.
  - pywa for WhatsApp.
  - django-mailbox for an email inbox.
- **Ideas only (GPL or AGPL):** ERPNext, Frappe Helpdesk, Snipe-IT. Vanna (text-to-SQL) is archived, so avoid it.
- **Costs (third-party sources, verify before quoting the client):**
  - WhatsApp inbound messages are free; utility templates cost about ₹0.115, marketing templates about ₹0.86.
  - A small LLM costs about ₹0.1–0.5 per message, so a few hundred rupees a month at this volume.

## Approaches Considered
### Approach A: Rules and alerts only
Morning digest, ageing colours, overdue batches, duplicate warnings and reorder suggestions, using rules only. Effort S (CC about 1 day). Low risk. Covers the "chasing" pain fully, but does little for typing and has no learning.

### Approach B: Three tiers, delivered in phases (CHOSEN, D7)
Tier 1 rules, tier 2 learning from history, tier 3 optional AI intake. Each phase is usable on its own. Effort L (CC about 8–9 days in total across P1–P6, human about 6–7 weeks). Medium risk, mostly in tier 3.

### Approach C: AI assistant first
Paste or forward a message or photo and get draft complaints, Stock IN from a DC photo, or a visit from a voice note. Effort M. High risk: blocked by the client's AI decision and Meta verification.

## Recommended Approach: B, three tiers in phases

### Architecture
- **New app `apps/insights/`** (tiers 1 and 2):
  - `rules.py`: thresholds and settings with sensible defaults, editable in the Django admin by Admin, one row per company where it matters.
  - `selectors.py`: every read (today list, ageing, duplicates, reorder suggestions, patterns).
  - `services.py`: only for its own writes (settings and the suggestion log). Accepting a suggestion calls the **existing** service, for example `inventory.services` to change a reorder level, so all write paths stay where they are.
- **New app `apps/intake/`** (tier 3 only):
  - Drafts, LLM calls, the usage ledger and the cost cap.
  - A draft is only ever opened in the **existing** complaint or entry form, pre-filled, so the existing validation, tokens (`guarded_post`) and services do the saving.
- **Scheduling:** a single management command, `send_daily_digest`, run once a day. In production a `scheduler` service in `compose.prod.yaml` runs the app image with supercronic (or a host cron entry); on the Windows dev machine, Task Scheduler. No Celery. The Today list itself is computed live when the page loads, so nothing needs refreshing in the background.
- **Fuzzy matching:** add the `pg_trgm` extension with a migration (`TrigramExtension`), plus GIN indexes on site name, item description and the master names.
- **Model setting:** the AI model name lives in a setting, `AI_MODEL`, not in code.
- **Data sufficiency gate:** each tier 2 feature declares its minimum history, for example 12 weeks and 4 non-zero weeks for an item forecast. Until then its UI shows a "learning" state instead of a guess.

### Tier 1: Rules (works from day one, no running cost)
| # | Feature | What the user sees | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **Today list** | A dashboard card and a `/today/` page with sections: overdue complaints, not attended for more than N days, pieces in store for more than 30 days with no batch, batches dispatched more than `Company.answer_days` ago and not settled, and **parts needed by open complaints that are short in stock** (`ComplaintNeed` compared with good stock). Each row links to its page. | Selectors over the existing models; it reuses the counts behind the menu badges. |
| 1.2 | **Morning email digest** (optional) | One email at 9:00 with the Today list, to the addresses Admin sets. Nothing is sent when the list is empty. | `send_daily_digest` command, Django `send_mail`, cron. |
| 1.3 | **Complaint due dates** (extends the existing ageing) | Today a complaint turns red after `LATE_DAYS = 15` (`apps/complaints/filters.py`), whatever its priority. This replaces that single number with a **due date per priority** (Urgent 1, High 2, Normal 4 working days, all editable): amber on the due day, red after it. `LATE_DAYS` stays only as the edge of the age-bucket filter. The list badge (`complaint_tags`) and the red rows in the pending-complaints export (`apps/complaints/exports.py`) switch to the due-date rule, so there is one meaning of "late". The existing `AGE_BUCKETS` filter and the paused clock stay as they are (`awaiting_days` in `apps/complaints/selectors.py`: time in Awaiting material does not count), so there is still one idea of "age". Adds a sort by "most overdue". | Working days used = working days from `reported_on` to today (or `closed_on`), minus the working days that fall inside Awaiting-material intervals (from `ComplaintEvent`). Sundays and `Holiday` rows are skipped. It is computed in Python over the open complaints (a few hundred at most), so "most overdue" is a Python sort, not a database sort. |
| 1.4 | **Duplicate warnings** | New site: "Did you mean *Punjab National Bank, Gudivada* (same phone)?" New complaint: "Open complaint CMP-0042 for this site, 12 days old. Add a visit there instead?" | Two separate rules. **Duplicate site:** an existing site with the same phone, OR (trigram similarity ≥ 0.6 on the name AND the same city). **Duplicate complaint:** an open complaint for the same site, filed in the last 30 days. Both warn; neither blocks. |
| 1.5 | **Typo guard for masters** (pairs with CR1) | Typing "Havels" in a type-to-add dropdown shows "Did you mean **Havells**?" above "＋ Add Havels". This answers the CR1 risk that typos are permanent. | Trigram or RapidFuzz on the master names inside `add_master`; the endpoint returns near matches. |
| 1.6 | **Fault-text reader (offline)** | On New complaint, typing "15watt Drivers 18nos not working" shows chips **Driver · 15 W · qty 18** and fills *Qty affected = 18*. On Record visit, the parts picker lists matching items first. | Regex and keyword rules (wattage `\d+\s*w(att)?`, quantity `\d+\s*(nos\|pcs)`, spare-type words and their plurals). Spare types are matched against the SpareType master names plus a small synonym table in code (for example drivers/drv → Driver, board/led module → PCB / LED module). The chips can add `ComplaintNeed` lines. All of them are hints that can be dismissed. |
| 1.7 | **Smart defaults** | Each user's last location is remembered; Stock OUT for a visit takes the location the parts came from last time; a new complaint for a known site pre-fills its contact and its last company. | Read from the last entries; nothing new is stored except one per-user preference. |
| 1.8 | **Entry sanity checks** | "18 is 9× the usual quantity for this item. Save anyway?" Also a warning for entries dated more than 30 days back, and when an adjustment would remove more than half the stock. | Median of the item's past lines, applied only once the item has at least 5 of them; a confirm step in the existing form, never a block. |

### Tier 2: Learning from history (switches on with enough data)
| # | Feature | What the user sees | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | **Reorder suggestions** | A "Suggested reorder level" column on Low stock and the item card, with an **Accept** button and the reasoning ("used 14 in 12 weeks, lead time 7 days, safety stock 3"). | **Consumption** = lines of OUT entries with subtype SITE, TECH, OTHER or COMPLAINT, minus lines of IN entries with subtype RETURN ("returned unused"). Entries that were undone, and the undo entries themselves, are left out. Transfers, adjustments, OPENING, SUPPLIER, REPLACEMENT and every Defective-store subtype do not count. **Formula, in days:** d = consumption over the last 84 days ÷ 84; L = `Company.lead_time_days`; σ = standard deviation of the 12 weekly totals (zero weeks included); suggested level = ⌈d × L + 1.65 × σ × √(L / 7)⌉. Items with at least 4 non-zero weeks that classify as intermittent (ADI > 1.32) use the SBA weekly rate ÷ 7 in place of d (hand-written, no new dependency). |
| 2.2 | **Dead and slow stock** | A report of items with stock but no use in 90 or 180 days, by location, with an export. | Selector modelled on ERPNext's Stock Ageing report. |
| 2.3 | **Fault patterns** | An Insights page showing repeat complaints at the same site within 90 days; failures by item, batch code and packed month ("Batch 24B: 7 drivers failed in 30 days"); rejection rate per company; average days for a company to answer. | GROUP BY selectors and ApexCharts, using the colour-blind-safe palette from DESIGN.md. |
| 2.4 | **Parts usually needed** | On Record visit: "For similar faults on this fitting, technicians usually used 2× driver X." | "Similar" means the same company plus the same spare-type and wattage chips from 1.6. It counts which items were used for those complaints, from the past visit OUT entries (subtype COMPLAINT). |
| 2.5 | **Technician hint** (nice to have, last in P5) | In the assign dropdown: "Ramu: 2 open complaints, visited this site before." | A simple score from open complaints assigned, earlier visits to the same site, and visits in the same city (the city comes from the sites the technician has visited, so no new field is needed). |
| 2.6 | **Legacy Excel import** | One-time `import_legacy` command with `--dry-run`, which writes a report of rows imported, cleaned and skipped (for example a bad phone like `88410`). | Replays the rows through the existing services, the way `seed_service_demo` does: items, opening stock, and **closed** historical complaints, so the pattern features have something to learn from. A row whose item or site can't be matched or created cleanly is skipped and listed in the report, never guessed. |

### Tier 3: Optional AI (only if the client approves the cost)
- **Switches:** `AI_ENABLED` (off by default) and `AI_MONTHLY_CAP_INR`. Every call is logged in an `AiUsage` table with tokens and estimated ₹. When the cap is reached, the AI buttons are hidden and Admin sees a note.
- **Model:** a small, fast Claude model (Haiku class, for example `claude-haiku-5-5`), set through `AI_MODEL`, with structured output validated by Pydantic. Every price in this doc is **to verify** before quoting the client.

| # | Feature | Flow |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | **Paste a message → draft complaint** | A "Paste message" box on New complaint. The extracted site, contact, phone, company, fault and quantity are checked against the masters (phone → known site, trigram on names), then **pre-fill the normal form**, with each AI-filled field marked. The raw text is saved with the complaint. |
| 3.2 | **Photo of a label or DC → draft** | Photo of a fitting label → item code (look-alike match through `search_key`). Photo of a DC → draft Stock IN lines. If the model reports "not readable", the field is left empty instead of guessed. |
| 3.3 | **Email inbox** | Forward the companies' complaint emails to an address. django-mailbox reads it over IMAP into a **Drafts** queue that staff open one by one. |
| 3.4 | **WhatsApp inbox** (only if the daily complaint count makes the Meta setup worth it) | WhatsApp Cloud API webhook (pywa) → the same Drafts queue. Needs Meta business verification and public HTTPS. Inbound messages are free. Optional utility-template replies such as "Complaint CMP-0042 registered" (about ₹0.115 each) are only ever sent when staff press Send. |

**Later, out of this spec:** asking questions in plain English. If it is ever built, it must go through a fixed set of selector "tools", never generated SQL, on a read-only database role.

## Success Criteria
- **Chasing:** the median working-day age of complaints at close, and the % of complaints closed by their due date, both compared with a baseline taken over the first 4 weeks after go-live. Also the % of return batches answered within `Company.answer_days`. Measured from the existing ComplaintEvent and DefectiveEvent history.
- **Typing:** duplicate-site and duplicate-complaint warnings accepted, and master typos caught by "Did you mean", both counted from `SuggestionEvent`. With tier 3: the share of complaints filed from a `Draft`, and the time from draft created to complaint saved.
- **Stock:** fewer complaints whose `ComplaintNeed` lines were short in stock when filed, the share of reorder suggestions accepted (`SuggestionEvent`), and dead stock quantity going down.
- **Trust:** zero writes made without a person confirming. An architecture test checks that the `insights` and `intake` selectors, rules and the digest command import no write service, and that every view that writes requires POST and uses `guarded_post`.
- Each phase: tests pass, ruff is clean, `makemigrations --check` is clean, and the walkthrough exits 0 with 0 JS errors.

## Distribution Plan
Today's `compose.yaml` is a development setup (runserver, dev dependencies); there is no production deployment file yet. New pieces:
- a production compose file, `compose.prod.yaml`: gunicorn, `config.settings.prod`, Postgres with a volume, and the scheduler below;
- a daily run of `send_daily_digest` (a `scheduler` service in `compose.prod.yaml` running supercronic, or a host cron entry);
- SMTP settings (`EMAIL_HOST`, `EMAIL_PORT`, `EMAIL_HOST_USER`, `EMAIL_HOST_PASSWORD`, `DEFAULT_FROM_EMAIL`) read from `.env` through django-environ; there are none today;
- the `pg_trgm` extension, created by a migration (the database user needs permission to create it);
- for tier 3 only: an API key in `.env`, outgoing HTTPS, and a public HTTPS webhook URL for WhatsApp.

## What I noticed about how you think
- You asked for "the entire system" to be intelligent, but when asked where it hurts you said "All the above". You see the whole workflow as connected, which is right for this app: a complaint, its stock and its defective pieces are one story.
- On spending money on AI you said "Ask the client first" instead of deciding for them. That protects the relationship and keeps the AI tier honest: it must earn its monthly cost.
- You agreed to "suggest, never act" straight away. In an app with an append-only stock ledger, that one rule is what keeps "intelligent" from becoming "unpredictable".
